**Decisions:**

- **The import is a verb on the API** (`import-client-assets --from <dir> [--dry-run]`), not a seeding
  flag and not a tool project. It needs the whole composition root — the configured media volumes with
  their startup validation, the real `IMediaService`, the database — and a seeding flag would tie the
  client's own photographs to `Seed__*`, which means "the shape a development database starts in". It
  never starts Kestrel, so it binds no port.
- **Every byte goes through `IMediaService`.** Nothing in the importer composes a `MediaAssets` row or
  touches a file, so each photograph gets the same signature check, EXIF stripping, rendition set,
  checksum deduplication and usage tracking as an admin upload.
- **The naming contract is a pure function in the Application layer**
  (`ClientAssetNaming`), separate from the importer that reads the disk. The rule that decides *which
  garment a photograph is of* is the one thing here that can go silently and expensively wrong, so it
  is testable exhaustively and without a database. Every repair is bounded by the real slug list; a
  near miss with two answers is refused rather than guessed; anything unplaceable stops the whole run
  before a byte is stored.
- **A product with one photograph gets a card and no hover.** Leaving the seeded placeholder in the
  hover slot would mean a real garment flipping, on hover, to a grey frame that says no photograph was
  supplied.
- **The ten kids' products keep their placeholders.** No adult photograph is put in front of a
  children's garment to fill a gap.

**Surprises worth the other person's time:**

- **Three filenames in the batch needed repairing, and that is the whole of the "several products have
  only one image" problem.** `ourt-canvas-sneakers-2` (a lost leading `c`),
  `productsleather-look-belt-1` and `productspique-polo-shirt-2` (the folder name run into the slug).
  Once resolved, coverage is **35 of 35 adult products with both photographs** and 0 of 10 kids. Every
  rename is printed by the import.
- **`hero.mp4` is H.264 and the pipeline took it — but it is 360 × 640 portrait.** The hero frame is
  16:9 from 768 px with `object-fit: cover`, so a 360 px-wide source is cropped to about a sixth of its
  height and upscaled roughly 5×. It works and it is soft. Ask the owner to re-export at 1920 × 1080.
- **The three logo files are not logos yet.** All three are **JPEGs** (2048 × 2048, `mode=RGB`, no alpha)
  with a `.png` name — the pipeline stored them as `image/jpeg`, which is the proof. `wordmark-dark`
  and `wordmark-light` are the *same mockup*: the mark embossed on a photographic concrete wall, with a
  drop shadow; their mean luminance differs by 1.9 of 255, so there is no white version for dark
  backgrounds. `monogram` is on a cream paper texture, so a favicon cut from it is a cream square. And
  the mark reads **COLOMBO WARDROBE**, where the codebase and the signed proposal say Spencer Denim.
  The three files are in the media library and the header is **unchanged**.
- **`activewear.jpg` is not activewear.** It is a flatlay of a white co-ord and a denim co-ord. It was
  imported into the slot its name names; the copy over it still says "Activewear".
- **A defect nothing could see until now: the hero and both banners stopped short of the right-hand
  edge at 1920** — the hero 1564 px of 1898, each banner 1680 px. CSS transfers a definite `max-height`
  through `aspect-ratio` into a **max-width** on an auto-width box (880 × 16/9 = 1564.44,
  720 × 21/9 = 1680, exactly what was measured). Fixed with an explicit `width: 100%`. It was invisible
  because those sections were seeded off, so no page had ever drawn one, and every hero test asked
  whether the element existed rather than how wide it was.
