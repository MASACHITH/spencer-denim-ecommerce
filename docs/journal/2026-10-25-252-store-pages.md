## What was built

### 1. The copy — `PolicyPageContent` (`AboutBody`, `PrivacyBody`, `TermsBody`)
Plain-English HTML in the shape the sanitiser already returns (`p`, `h2`, `ul`, `li`, `strong`; no links, no attributes, no table), so a body is stored exactly as written and the public API returns it unchanged.

| Privacy policy | 1,051 | When you order or pay · If you have an account · When you write to us or join the newsletter · When you browse · Cookies and your browser · Who receives your data · How long we keep your data · Keeping your data safe · Your rights · Changes to this policy |

- **The Privacy policy is over the brief's 600–900 words** (it was cut from 1,035 to 952 and then grew when review found data the code collects that the first draft did not say). Every addition is something a privacy policy should say; the owner or lawyer can trim wording, not these facts. The guard allows 600–1,120.
- **The shop name is `StoreBrand.Name`**, interpolated into a constant string (C# 10), so `BrandNameConventionTests` still passes and no raw brand literal exists in code. The company name is a private constant in the same file; it is the client's legal name, not the shop's, and `Store.LegalName` is unset (`SettingKeys.cs:75`, `SettingCatalog.cs:239`), so it is unconfirmed.
- **No link, address, phone number, email, fee or return window** is written in any of the three, and **no page is named by its seeded title**: the copy says "our delivery page", "our returns page", "the Contact page", because a title or slug is editable by the client.
- **Privacy** is written under Sri Lanka's Personal Data Protection Act, No. 9 of 2022 (named in `docs/01-project-understanding.md:37`). Overseas customers are covered by saying that the same practices and rights apply to everyone.
- **Terms** say PayPal is available for every order and is the way to pay for a delivery outside Sri Lanka; bank transfer may also be offered for a delivery within Sri Lanka (owner decision 2026-10-24, issue **#253**, built in parallel). **Until #253 lands the Terms describe a shop one change ahead of `main`**: `main` offers PayPal only, so nothing in them is false, but "we may also offer payment by bank transfer" is about a method that is switched off.

### 2. How it reaches a database — the #235 mechanism, extended without changing its rules
`HomeContentSeeder.SeedPolicyPagesAsync` offers the policy copy **once per `Seed.PolicyPagesVersion`**, fills and **publishes** a page only while its body is **empty or exactly an earlier seed**, and never overwrites anything else. Changes, in `PolicyPageContent` and `HomeContentSeeder`:

1. **`SeedVersion` 1 → 2.** A database that recorded version 1 (UAT, anything that ran #235) is offered the new copy on its next start; a fresh database goes straight to 2. Idempotent: a second start finds the marker at 2 and does nothing.
2. **`IntroducedIn` per page** (Shipping 1, Returns 1, About 2, Privacy 2, Terms 2). A page is offered only if its `IntroducedIn` is **greater than the recorded version**. *Why:* a plain version bump would have re-offered Shipping and Returns, and an admin who had emptied or unpublished one after v1 would have had it refilled and republished. Tested by `Only_the_five_policy_pages_are_seeded_with_copy_and_each_has_a_version_it_was_introduced_in` and by `A_database_that_took_version_one_gets_only_the_three_new_pages`, which empties and unpublishes Returns as an admin would, winds the marker back to 1 and checks the upgrade fills only About, Privacy and Terms and leaves Returns alone.
3. **`UpdatedAtUtc` is stamped on every page the seeder fills** (round 2). The page shows "Last updated" from `UpdatedAtUtc ?? PublishedAtUtc` (`content-page.ts:240`), and `SetPublished` stamps `PublishedAtUtc` only the first time, so a page published empty long ago would have shown that old date over brand-new copy. The same test gives About an old `PublishedAtUtc` and checks the old stamp is kept and `UpdatedAtUtc` is new.
4. **`PreviousSeeds` / `Bodies`** carry five keys; `PreviousSeeds` is what makes a later copy edit safe.

**What happens to an admin's choices** (integration tests in `DeliveryReturnsSeedTests`, real SQL Server):

| State of the page when the upgrade runs | Result |
|---|---|
| Body empty, any publish flag (the install default: unpublished) | Filled and published |
| Body holds the admin's own words | Untouched, published flag untouched (`On_a_database_that_took_version_one_an_admins_words_survive_and_an_empty_page_is_filled`) |
| Body empty because the admin **emptied** it before the upgrade, still unpublished | Indistinguishable from a fresh install: **filled and published** (no column records "an admin emptied this"). Documented in the seeder |
| Body emptied or unpublished by an admin **after** the marker is at 2 | Left alone for ever (`After_the_first_offer_an_admin_who_unpublishes_or_empties_a_new_page_keeps_it_that_way`) |
| Page soft-deleted | Not offered |
| `Contact` | Never receives copy (`SystemPageCopyTests`) |
| Shipping / Returns, any state | Never re-offered by this change |

### 3. Consent points
- **Checkout** — "By placing this order you agree to our terms and conditions." (plain words while the page is unpublished, as the shop has always said it), and, when published, a second sentence "How we use your details is explained in our privacy policy." Both links open in a **new tab**.
- **Register** — "By creating an account you agree to our terms and conditions." and the same privacy sentence; no sentence at all when neither page is published. New tab.
- **Newsletter** — the homepage section carried `TODO(GLB-10)`; the privacy policy is linked when published ("Read our privacy policy."), in the **same tab** (nothing typed there is lost by leaving).
- All use `sd-legal-links` (`ui/legal-links/legal-links.ts`): links come from the published-page index **by system key** so a renamed slug cannot break them; the words are the page title in lower case in every state; `target` is bound through RouterLink's own input (RouterLink owns that host binding, and a plain `[attr.target]` was measured to be overwritten).
- **Why a new tab at checkout and register:** the form is component state (`checkout-page.ts` form signal, the register page's signals) with no draft and no `canDeactivate`, so a link followed in the same tab would throw away everything typed. The links are inline in a sentence; the 44 px floor does not apply (comment in the component).

### 4. The newsletter gap (found in review; not built here)
Nothing in the shop implements unsubscribing. `NewsletterSubscriber.UnsubscribeToken` is generated (`NewsletterSubscriptions.cs:45`) and used nowhere; `UnsubscribedAtUtc` (`NewsletterSubscriber.cs:40`) is set to `null` on re-subscribing (`:54`) and set by nothing; there is no endpoint, no link and no admin action. A separate issue will build it. Until then:
- the footer's and the homepage's sign-up lines say "You can ask us to stop at any time through the Contact page." (they said "Unsubscribe from any email" / "…of them"), with a comment at each saying why;
- the Privacy policy says to stop the newsletter, or to withdraw any other consent, you write to us through the Contact page, and "When you ask us to stop the newsletter, we remove you from the list." That is an **operational promise**: staff must do it by hand, in the database, because no screen does it. It is on the owner's list;
- tests pin the wording (`footer`, `home-page.spec`, `StorePagesCopyTests.No_page_promises_a_self_service_unsubscribe_the_shop_does_not_have`), and the lint forbids the word "unsubscribe" in these pages, so the day the feature ships the tests are what say the copy can change.
